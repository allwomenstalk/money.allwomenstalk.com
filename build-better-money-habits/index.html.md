# How to Build Better Money Habits Without Overhauling Your Entire Life

You don’t have to put your life on hold to start improving your finances. In fact, a few changes to your current way of life can have a big impact over time, as long as you make them a habit. You don’t have to aim for perfection. Striving to make better financial decisions and stick to them long-term is enough.

By allowing yourself to make ‘good’ financial decisions on a regular basis, you will find that these begin to support your current way of living as well as your future financial goals.

## Start by Observing Your Current Spending

Before you can change how you approach your finances, it helps to get a grip on how you currently spend your money. By [recording your transactions](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/how-should-i-record-my-business-transactions) for several weeks, you will get a clear picture of your spending habits, including recurring charges, unexpected purchases, and areas where your money goes that no longer reflect how you have grown as a person.

For example, recognizing that you spend a lot of money on eating out every week might help you to cut back on this non-essential expense and free up more money each month. On the other hand, recognizing that you enjoy buying occasional presents for your friends may help you to include this in your spending plan too.

## Choose One Financial Goal at a Time

Don’t try to tackle everything at once – pick one thing to aim for, and focus on that one first. Build an emergency fund, tackle credit card debt, or save for something specific like a holiday. That way, you can make progress toward your goal and start developing a few good habits along the way.

Another reason to have one financial goal at a time is that it gives your finances more focus. With a clear focus on what you are trying to achieve with your finances, you can make better financial decisions. As you get used to one financial habit, you can move on to another financial goal.

## Automate the Habits You Want to Maintain

Automating good habits, including your savings, can make a big difference. This can include transferring small amounts of money into your savings account every few days or paying bills automatically on the due date.

Start with an amount you can easily transfer regularly, then increase it over time as your income or circumstances allow.

## Make Credit Awareness Part of Your Routine

The credit you use and the way you manage your payments can have a huge bearing on your ability to get financing, a mortgage, or other things, like homes and cars. By keeping track of your credit, you can see how even something as simple as timely payment of bills can affect your overall credit profile.

Using [free credit monitoring](https://www.sofi.com/coach/credit-score-monitoring/) can make this habit easier to maintain. It provides a convenient way to follow changes in your credit score and remain aware of your progress without adding a time-consuming task to your schedule. A brief, regular review can help you stay connected to this important part of your financial health.

## Create Simple Rules for Everyday Spending

Personal financial rules help reduce the number of financial decisions we have to make each day. For example, waiting 24 hours before making an impulse purchase, setting a weekly allowance for discretionary spending, or unsubscribing from marketing messages that trigger spending are all examples of simple rules to live by.

Whether you set tight rules or prefer more flexibility, your personal rules should help you. So choose your rules carefully to strike the right balance of direction and freedom. When a rule is easy to follow, it usually becomes a habit quickly.

## Build a Small Financial Buffer

Even small amounts can go a long way when an unexpected expense comes up. An emergency fund does not have to be large at the beginning. Start with a realistic target. Donate the amount you save each month to a practical fund until you build up enough for unexpected expenses.

Contributing small amounts of money to a savings account over time can create a financial safety net. The initial target amount should be manageable, as the funds will be added to over time, eventually reaching a suitable level to cater for any unexpected occurrences in your life.

## Review Your Progress Without Judging Yourself

Tracking your progress doesn't have to feel negative. Set aside time each month to review how you have done during the month in terms of your spending, saving, bank balances, and any credit you may have. Note what happened and be honest about why you didn't reach your target.

The point of a review is to gather information and go from there, not to test your willpower or ability to keep to a set of habits. If your review doesn’t show the progress you wanted, review why, make any necessary changes, and keep going.

## Let Better Habits Grow With Your Life

You will also need to [revise your habits](https://medium.com/@monalazzar/the-6-tiny-habits-that-will-make-you-financially-literate-by-the-end-of-the-year-976ec0802afb) as your life circumstances change. Therefore, a new job, moving to a new home, or a change in family circumstances will also require changes in the amount you save and the amount of spending you allow. Keep improving once you have established good habits.

As your money habits evolve and improve, they can become even better. Gradually increase the amount you save each month, redirect any extra income towards your outstanding debts or begin planning for long-term goals.

## Make Improvement Easy to Repeat

Most new habits develop through a series of small changes over time. Unless you are extremely fortunate, turning around your spending habits is unlikely to happen overnight. However, monitoring your spending, automating positive financial habits, checking your credit rating, and tracking your progress over time can eventually become part of your routine and give you greater control over your finances.

Begin with one change you can implement in your life right now. As you get used to this habit, you can add more changes until you see the improvements you want in your life.
